## Demystifying the Complex Role of Hydropower and Agricultural Production in the Asian Green Revolution

The rapid and global expansion of dam infrastructure, together with recent high-profile flooding and crop loss events linked to hydropower operations, has raised concerns about the trade-offs these systems impose on downstream agriculture. This study presents a multi-decadal, data-driven assessment of downstream agricultural responses to hydropower reservoirs across 210 sites located in developing countries central to the Asian Green Revolution. It introduces the Agricultural Impact Score (AIS), a composite, anomaly-based metric that integrates satellite observations, climate reanalysis, modeled reservoir outflows, and newly available irrigation network information from the Global Registry of Agricultural Irrigation Networks (GRAIN) to quantify downstream hydro-agricultural impacts. Results reveal substantial heterogeneity and a clear asymmetry between benefit and failure-driven pathways. Across the study site ensemble, 86.1% of hydropower dams exhibit net positive AIS, while 13.9% show net negative AIS values. Positive impacts are spatially widespread and primarily associated with growing-season productivity gains and effective flow regulation, whereas severe negative impacts are localized and concentrated in a small subset of reservoirs, several of which correspond to documented cases of downstream flooding and crop losses. Nearly 40% of sites that experienced dam-driven flood events in the study region were associated with delayed post-flood agricultural productivity boosts, indicating beneficial sediment deposition effects on downstream croplands. Attribution analyses further indicate that agricultural productivity is more frequently coupled with reservoir outflow variability than with precipitation alone. Notably, India and China, two major agricultural producers in the region, exhibit distinct patterns. Hydropower dams in India are overwhelmingly associated with positive agricultural outcomes, whereas China shows comparatively lower prevalence of positive impacts with greater variability, consistent with stronger hydropower and multipurpose operational priorities.Together, these findings demonstrate that hydropower reservoirs more often enhance than impair downstream agricultural productivity, while underscoring the importance of context-specific operation strategies to mitigate flood-related risks.
